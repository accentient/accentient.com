One of my non-technical enjoyments in life is listening to the This American Life podcast, from Public Radio International. It isn’t always my political cup of tea but it is always entertaining.

I was catching up on some past shows recently, when I ran across this episode:
<blockquote><a href="http://thislife.org/Radio_Episode.aspx?sched=1275" target="_blank" rel="noopener">December 19, 2008: "Ruining it For the Rest of Us."</a></blockquote>
The prologue to this episode is the first 13 minutes and is a wonderful discussion of team dynamics. I strongly suggest downloading and listening to it.

From the This American Life website:
<blockquote>A bad apple, at least at work, can spoil the whole barrel. And there's research to prove it. Host Ira Glass talks to <a href="http://www.rsm.nl/home/faculty/academic_departments/organisation_and_personnel_management/faculty/faculty/felps">Will Felps</a>, a professor at Rotterdam School of Management in the Netherlands, who designed an experiment to see what happens when a bad worker joins a team. Felps divided people into small groups and gave them a task. One member of the group would be an actor, acting either like a jerk, a slacker or a depressive. And within 45 minutes, the rest of the group started behaving like the bad apple.</blockquote>
How many of us have met one of these people on a team we have worked in? How many of us have committed the sin of being one of these people at some point in our careers?
<h2>The Jerk</h2>
The jerk is someone who attacks or insults others. The jerk is a voice of derision and operates by tearing down others. Tag lines of the jerk include:
<ul>
	<li>Are you kidding me?</li>
	<li>Have you ever actually taken a  course?</li>
	<li>Do you have any idea what you’re doing?</li>
	<li>Lots of eye rolls</li>
</ul>
<h2>The Slacker</h2>
The slacker consistently endeavors to do less work. Typical slacker behaviors include:
<ul>
	<li>Lean back, feet up</li>
	<li>Texting another person in a meeting</li>
	<li>Commonly says, "Whatever."</li>
	<li>Often overheard saying, "I don’t care."</li>
	<li>Will ultimately be heard claiming, "This job doesn’t matter. Let’s just get it done."</li>
	<li>Lots of eye rolls</li>
</ul>
<h2>The Depressive Pessimist</h2>
A depressive pessimist is a doom and gloomer. This is the person on the team most often nicknamed "Eyore". Common Eyore traits include:
<ul>
	<li>Head down on the table/desk</li>
	<li>State that the effort is unenjoyable</li>
	<li>Overheard saying, "This work won’t matter when we’re done anyway."</li>
	<li>Body language slackens and hunches down</li>
	<li>Lots of eye rolls</li>
</ul>
<h2>Some bad apple statistics</h2>
The following statistical findings were claimed in the study discussed in the segment.
<ul>
	<li>A team with a bad apple member will perform 30-40% worse than a team with no bad apples.</li>
	<li>The presence of a bad apple on the team results in less communication between others.</li>
	<li>People mirror bad behavior in working with others.</li>
</ul>
And finally, it comes to this: A team isn’t often elevated by its best member, but depressed by its worst.

Wow.
