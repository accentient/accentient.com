As I have <a href="http://elegantcode.com/2008/09/27/changing-source-control-as-a-kaizen-event/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">stated before</a>, I am amazed at how an organizations culture (read: dysfunctions or strengths) are reflected in their source control systems. This is a really a restatement of <a href="http://www.jnd.org/dn.mss/design_as_pract.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Don Norman's assertion</a>, which says, "The design of a software system reflects the culture of the organization that created it."

So, not to belabor a point, but can anyone really make a case for a SCC model that is not a <a href="http://labs.seapine.com/wiki/index.php/Surround_SCM_Branching_Strategies#Branch_By_Purpose" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Branch-by-Purpose model</a>? Here's my guess. If you literally are unable to use this model in SCC to ship your software, I believe I can find a smell that is causing it.

Thoughts?
