I was recently reading Geoff Koch's article in <a href="http://www.stpmag.com/" target="none" rel="noopener">Software Test and Performance&nbsp;magazine</a> on <em>SCM: More Than Code Check-In, Check-Out</em>. It was a short, but good&nbsp;article talking about how CM is similar regardless of whether you write articles or software. I would agree. I believe that i just&nbsp;becomes a matter of complexity.

I found a couple of interesting points in his article:
<ul>
 	<li><a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/" target="none" rel="noopener">Wikipedia</a> has an exhaustive <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_revision_control_software" target="none" rel="noopener">list</a> of revision control software.</li>
 	<li>Money magazine lists software engineer as No. 1 in its <a href="http://money.cnn.com/magazines/moneymag/bestjobs/index.html" target="none" rel="noopener">Best Jobs in America</a> (which I knew), and that release engineers are hailed as having the top-paying jobs (which I didn't know).</li>
 	<li>Google's Dan Bloch recently stated that they have more than 3,000 users accessing a single Perforce repository running on an HP 4-way Opteron server with 128gb. Wowsers!</li>
</ul>
Side note: have you checked out <a href="http://code.google.com" target="none" rel="noopener">Google Code</a>? Apparently, Google will crawl publicly accessible source code, such as (.tar, .gz, .tar, .bz2, and .zip archives) and&nbsp;CVS and Subversion repositories.
