Last week, Microsoft <a href="http://www.microsoft.com/presspass/press/2008/sep08/09-10OMGModelingPR.mspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">announced</a> that they had joined the <a href="http://www.omg.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Object Management Group</a>. OMG is the consortium responsible for many distributed, and object-oriented specifications. One of their sets of standards defines the Unified Modeling Language (<a href="http://www.uml.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">UML</a>), and I'm sure that's the reason Microsoft joined the ranks.



Knowing what's coming in the <a href="http://msdn.microsoft.com/en-us/vstudio/bb725993.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Rosario</a> (and beyond) versions of Visual Studio Team System, I'm glad to see this happening, as it reinforces that Microsoft is taking their modeling strategy to the mainstream.
