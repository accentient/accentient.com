At Accentient, we are always running into clients who need to implement a methodology, and have it checked for 'correctness' as time goes along.&nbsp; With TFS, as it stands, correctness checks need to be made in one of three ways:<br /><br />1)&nbsp; TFS has a built in checking mechanism.&nbsp; This generally occurs INSIDE a work item.&nbsp; For instance, Field A can only be changed by a Project Manager.&nbsp; This is the BEST mechanism, since you can ensure validity prior to committing the work item changes to TFS.<br /><br />2)&nbsp; Listen to the available events (Work Item Save, for instance), and react to the event.&nbsp; This is possible when we're doing things like ensuring that every "Requirement" has an associated "Risk" associated with it (mandatory for regulatory compliance in health care research).&nbsp; This is a GOOD mechanism, as the project may go out of compliance, but the violation is noted immediately.<br /><br />3)&nbsp; Verify the model at periodic intervals, either on demand or scheduled.&nbsp; Here you would simply use automation to query TFS and ensure compliance with the methodology.&nbsp; This is a SO-SO method, since the project goes in an out of compliance throughout the project lifecycle.<br /><br />Some people may claim that Check-in Policies may be used to ensure client-side compliance.&nbsp; That is true, but only to a limited degree.&nbsp; The check-in policies are run only when something is being checked into TFS, not when a work item is saved.<br /><br />Another claim may be that we could use VSIP to extend Visual Studio.&nbsp; This is indeed the case, and would provide the greatest amount of validation, however, in most cases not everyone who interacts with TFS is using VS2005 - some use Project, Excel, Word, Outlook, TeamPlain, TeamPrise, etc.<br /><br />So, what I'd personally like:<br /><br />1)&nbsp; Events around everything that happens in TFS, including very granular things like a Work Item get.&nbsp; I know this may have performance implications, but a big, hairy warning could be affixed to the VSIP SDK, and we could extend at our own risk.&nbsp; In a vast majority of the cases I work with, the machines used to power TFS are so much in excess of recommended load that I have, literally, gigabytes of memory and maybe 50-75% of the processor to play with.<br /><br />2)&nbsp; Client side Work Item Check-in Policies.&nbsp; Let me interrupt, on the client-side, a work item save and do validation before I allow it to be committed to the server.<br /><br />3)&nbsp; An SDK or a prebuild web service, if you will, that I can install that will 'catch' the thrown events, and translate them into something intelligible, like a clean object. &nbsp;<br /><br />Over the course of the next few weeks, I'2013-08-28 13:43:09'll be posting more about thoughts of validating and enforcing methodologies using TFS.<br /><br />
