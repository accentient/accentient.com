<p>Thanks to my fellow Visual Studio ALM MVP <a href="http://www.colinsalmcorner.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Colin Dembovsky</a> for building the “One Work Item” check-in policy that I was always too lazy to build.</p> <p><img title="image" style="display: block; max-width: 100%; height: auto; margin-bottom: 1rem; border: 1px solid black; box-shadow: 2px 2px 10px rgba(0,0,0,0.5);" border="0" alt="image" src="image1.png" width="321" height="207"></p> <p>Judging by the title, it may sound like one of the other work item related check-in policies provided by Microsoft, but this one is different. It allows the team to select the <em>type</em> of work item required to be associated with a check-in, as well as the <em>count</em> (exactly 1 or at least 1).</p> <p><img title="image" style="display: block; max-width: 100%; height: auto; margin-bottom: 1rem; border: 1px solid black; box-shadow: 2px 2px 10px rgba(0,0,0,0.5);" border="0" alt="image" src="image2.png" width="304" height="154"></p> <p>For <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuhari" target="_blank" rel="noopener">shu-level</a> teams new to using the traceability features in Team Foundation Server, I recommend being “chatty” with their check-ins, rather than “chunky”. In other words, as a developer works on tasks throughout the day, he or she should check-in against the related task as needed, rather than at the end of the day (or the week) against several task work items. </p> <p>You can download this check-in policy, along with other useful ones that Colin has built, at the Visual Studio Gallery for <a href="http://visualstudiogallery.msdn.microsoft.com/c476b708-77a8-4065-b9d0-919ab688f078" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Visual Studio 2012</a> or <a href="http://visualstudiogallery.msdn.microsoft.com/3bd2115e-9009-414b-bb5b-a0a64e4dad9e" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Visual Studio 2013</a>.</p>
